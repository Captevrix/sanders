# Sanders Housing — Manufactured Home Buyer Personas

Pensacola / Gulf Coast dealer. Research basis: the live sandershousing.com inventory
and navigation, CFPB HMDA manufactured-housing findings, Pew 2025 manufactured-home
financing research, and IPA's 2025 manufactured housing national report.

## Category truths that outrank aesthetics

1. **This category is shopped by monthly payment, not sticker price.** Most buyers
   qualify around a payment, then work backward to a home.
2. **Land ownership is the fork that determines everything.** Home-only (chattel)
   loans carry higher rates and shorter terms than land-home loans; CFPB data shows
   the land question drives cost more than the home does.
3. **Credit anxiety is the #1 reason a shopper never calls.** Roughly 1 in 5 buyers
   end up in risky contract financing, and many assume they will be rejected.
4. **On the Gulf Coast, storm safety is a purchase requirement.** Wind Zone II/III
   ratings, tie-downs, and HUD code compliance are not fine print here.
5. **Traffic is mobile-dominant and phone-conversion-driven.** The dealership call is
   the conversion event; the site's job is to get a qualified caller, not a signup.
6. **Real photography sells; abstraction does not.** Buyers want to see the actual
   home on the actual lot, plus the interior they will walk.

## Persona 1 — "Payment-First" First-Time Buyer

**Who:** 26–40, household income $45k–$75k, currently renting in Escambia/Santa Rosa
County. Often a couple with kids. Credit 580–680. No land.

**How they shop:** Phone, at night. Searches "mobile homes near me with payments",
"bad credit mobile home Pensacola", "how much down for a mobile home".

**Fears:** Being denied and embarrassed. Hidden costs — delivery, setup, A/C, steps,
skirting, lot rent. Signing something predatory.

**Blockers to calling:** No prices on the site. No payment estimate. No idea if they
qualify. Feeling they'll be handed to a salesperson before they understand anything.

**What converts them:** "$X/mo est." on every home. A soft pre-qualification that
promises no credit-score hit. A plain-English breakdown of what's included in the
price. Financing partners named up front. Real customer stories from people like them.

**Design implications:** Payment prominence equal to or above price. "See if you
qualify" as a top-level action. Total-cost transparency block. Persistent tap-to-call.

## Persona 2 — "Sun and Simplicity" Retiree / Downsizer

**Who:** 58–75, relocating to or already in the Gulf Coast. Selling a stick-built home
elsewhere; frequently paying cash or putting 40%+ down. Often placing the home in a
55+ community or on a small parcel.

**How they shop:** Desktop and tablet, daytime, long sessions. Browses floor plans
repeatedly before contacting. Searches by model name, square footage, and "3 bedroom
2 bath double wide floor plans".

**Fears:** Buying something flimsy. Hurricane exposure. Resale value. Being sold a
larger home than they need. Complicated setup they have to manage themselves.

**Blockers to calling:** No floor plan drawings. No specs (wind zone, roof load,
insulation, dimensions). No sense of the company's longevity or the people involved.

**What converts them:** Downloadable/zoomable floor plans, honest interior galleries,
spec tables, wind-zone and warranty facts, a page with the actual staff and years in
business, and single-level / low-maintenance framing.

**Design implications:** Floor-plan-forward listing detail. Spec table as a first-class
component. Larger type and high contrast (this cohort skews presbyopic). Trust and
storm-safety evidence sections. Calm, warm, non-trendy visual register.

## Persona 3 — "I Own the Land" Replacement Buyer

**Who:** 35–65, owns rural or semi-rural acreage in Escambia, Santa Rosa, Baldwin, or
Okaloosa county. Replacing an aging or storm-damaged unit, or placing a home for
family. Frequently pays with a land-home loan or insurance proceeds.

**How they shop:** Mixed device, urgent timeline. Searches by dimensions ("28x60"),
sections, and "mobile home setup and delivery Pensacola".

**Fears:** The home won't fit or won't be permitted. Setup, permitting, septic, power,
and tie-downs becoming their problem. Delivery timeline slipping.

**Blockers to calling:** No dimensions on listings. No statement of what the dealer
handles vs. what the buyer handles. No delivery radius or lead time.

**What converts them:** Dimensions and sections listed on the card, not buried. A
clear "what we handle" list (delivery, set, tie-down, skirting, permitting support).
A stated service radius and realistic timeline. In-stock / on-display availability.

**Design implications:** Dimensions + sections as primary card metadata. A "Do you own
land?" fork early in the journey that routes to a land-home path. Delivery/setup
services section. Availability status ("On our lot now") as a badge that means something.

## Weighting for the redesign

Persona 1 drives volume and is the most under-served by the current site — payment and
qualification content leads. Persona 2 drives average sale price — floor plans and
spec/trust depth. Persona 3 drives fast closes — dimensions, availability, and setup
services. The homepage must serve all three within one scroll by forking early on
**payment**, **floor plan**, and **land**.

## Requirements the design must satisfy

- Estimated monthly payment shown on every home card, alongside price.
- "See if you qualify" / financing elevated to primary navigation and hero.
- An early "Do you own land?" fork.
- Search reduced to what buyers say: beds, sections, sqft, price/payment band.
- Wind zone, warranty, setup-included and years-in-business evidence on the homepage.
- Floor plan access from the listing card or detail page.
- Mobile-first: persistent tap-to-call, large touch targets, fast images.
- Real home photography over illustration or abstract editorial treatments.
